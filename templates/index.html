Sheeter is for people who know their note names but cannot sight-read. Photograph a line of music and it tells you, step by step, which notes are in the chord under each hand.

What makes a good photo:

  • One system, or one page. Not a whole open book.
  • Hold the page flat and the phone parallel to it. Sheeter straightens small tilts, not curl.
  • Fill the frame with the music, and keep your shadow off it.
% end

What this does

Sheeter finds the staff lines in your photo, measures where every notehead sits against them, and turns those positions into pitches using the clef and key signature it reads off the front of each staff. Then it names the chord each group of notes makes.

% if verify_available:

Every reading is measured this way first. You can then ask Claude to look at the photo alongside it and check the hard parts, from the button on the reading itself. Each reading says plainly whether that has happened.

% else:

Set an Anthropic API key and you also get a button to have Claude look at the photo and check the reading. Without one you get the measured reading, which is what the app is.

% end

It is not a transcription tool. There is no rhythm beyond hollow-or-filled, and no output file. It answers one question: what notes am I looking at here?
